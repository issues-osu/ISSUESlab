abstract: "Exclusionary discipline disproportionately affects youth of color, but Indigenous student experiences remain underexplored. Using 2019–2020 New Mexico K–12 public school data, we applied random intercept multilevel modeling to examine the impact of student race/ethnicity and infraction severity on the odds of receiving harsh exclusionary discipline (out-of-school suspension or referral to law enforcement) versus in-school suspension. White students had significantly lower odds of receiving harsh discipline compared to Native American students. Furthermore, while the odds of harsh discipline increased for all students as infraction severity increased, the odds of harsh discipline declined much more sharply for White, Black, and Latine students than for Native American students as infraction severity decreased—demonstrating that Native American students face disproportionately harsher consequences for minor infractions."
