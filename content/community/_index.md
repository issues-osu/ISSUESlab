## Our Mission

The ISSUES Lab is a transdisciplinary research group that applies advanced spatial statistics, geospatial artificial intelligence (GeoAI), and machine learning to understand how neighborhood environments shape health, safety, and social well-being. By integrating large-scale administrative, environmental, health, and geospatial datasets, we generate evidence that informs public health policy, legal decision-making, and community interventions.

Our mission is to do applied work that impacts social policy by leveraging data and recent advances in spatial and statistical analysis. We are particularly interested in the intersection of the social and physical environment and its impact on child and adolescent well-being broadly defined. This includes adverse childhood experiences, child abuse and neglect, and other experiences of trauma.

---

## Scholar-Activist Identity

We are a multidisciplinary team of scholar-activists from diverse backgrounds with a shared research goal of strengthening families by highlighting their assets while addressing barriers to health. We are united around our passion for improving the lives of children and families.

Our research is grounded in the recognition that where people live fundamentally influences health and opportunity. We investigate how structural and environmental conditions—including neighborhood deprivation, environmental hazards, housing instability, land use, transportation access, climate-related risks, surveillance, and the built environment—contribute to health disparities and involvement with child welfare, healthcare, and the justice system. Using advanced analytical approaches, including Bayesian spatial modeling, multilevel modeling, spatial epidemiology, geospatial machine learning, and foundation models for satellite imagery, we identify geographic patterns of risk and resilience across communities.

Our work spans environmental justice, housing justice, child and family well-being, injury and violence prevention, mental health, and climate-related health inequities. We translate complex spatial data into actionable evidence for policymakers, public health practitioners, attorneys, judges, and community organizations, developing data-driven strategies that promote healthier, safer, and more equitable communities.

## Humanizing Language Framework

Language is important. We no longer use terms that dehumanize individuals, families, and communities. This means that:
* We do not refer to people as *delinquents*.
* We do not refer to households as *dysfunctional*.
* We do not refer to communities as *disorganized*.

Instead, our work focuses on highlighting community assets, structural factors, and neighborhood-level solutions.

---

## The 4Cs Philosophy

Our group follows the **4Cs** philosophy to guide our day-to-day research, mentorship, and engagement:

1. **Culture**: Respecting and understanding the diverse cultural contexts of the communities we study and work with.
2. **Community**: Emphasizing community as a critical source for social change and centering community voices in our analysis.
3. **Collaboration**: Engaging in thoughtful, transdisciplinary collaboration across social work, public health, law, and data science.
4. **Communication**: Presenting complex spatial and statistical data in accessible, clear formats for lawyers, judges, policymakers, and the public.

## ISSUES Lab Community Values & Expectations

### Our Community

The ISSUES Lab is more than a research group—we are a community of scholars who learn from one another, support one another, and work together to conduct rigorous, meaningful research that advances social and environmental justice.

Our shared goal is to produce impactful science while creating an environment where people enjoy coming to work, feel supported, and continue to grow as researchers and as people. Research can be demanding, but it should also be rewarding. We believe that our health, well-being, families, and relationships are more important than any publication or grant. We strive to work hard, have fun, celebrate successes, and support one another through challenges.

### Our Shared Values

Every member of the ISSUES Lab contributes to the culture we create together. We are committed to:

- Treating one another with kindness, respect, and professionalism.
- Approaching collaboration with gratitude and humility.
- Choosing curiosity over ego.
- Supporting one another's success rather than competing with one another.
- Sharing knowledge freely and helping others grow.
- Assuming good intentions while communicating honestly and respectfully.

We recognize that disagreements and mistakes are a normal part of research. What matters is how we respond—with openness, professionalism, and a commitment to finding solutions together.

---

### Collaboration & Authorship

Collaboration depends on trust, transparency, and open communication.

When we collaborate on a project:

- The first author serves as the project lead and is responsible for moving the work forward.
- Authorship should reflect meaningful intellectual and scholarly contributions.
- Conversations about authorship should occur early and continue throughout the project as contributions evolve.
- Before submission, the first author should ensure that all collaborators understand and agree with the proposed authorship order whenever possible.

When disagreements arise, we encourage collaborators to discuss them directly, respectfully, and professionally. These conversations are an important part of becoming independent researchers.

Because many lab members collaborate across multiple projects, routine authorship discussions should remain among the project team rather than involving the lab director. This helps maintain fairness and avoids placing one person in the position of deciding among collaborators. If a serious ethical concern arises, we will address it together.

When someone develops an original research idea, that person leads the project. They establish the vision, coordinate the work, and make authorship decisions after thoughtful discussion with collaborators. These expectations apply equally to everyone in the lab.

---

### Mentorship & Opportunities

We recognize that not everyone has the same role within the lab.

Faculty members have formal mentoring responsibilities to dissertation advisees, committee students, graduate research assistants, and other trainees. As a result, opportunities, mentoring time, and project priorities may differ depending on these responsibilities.

These differences are not a reflection of anyone's value or potential. Rather, they reflect the commitments that accompany different roles and ensure that everyone receives the mentorship they have been promised.

At the same time, we are committed to helping every member of the lab develop their own ideas, build collaborations, publish their work, and achieve their professional goals.

---

### Commitment to the Community

Members participate in the ISSUES Lab in different capacities, including volunteers, students earning course credit, graduate research assistants, dissertation students, and collaborators.

Different roles naturally involve different expectations, responsibilities, and levels of commitment.

One expectation, however, is shared by everyone: **participation**.

We ask each member to attend lab meetings whenever possible, communicate when conflicts arise, remain engaged in collaborative activities, and contribute positively to the community.

If someone accepts a funded or formal position but is consistently unable to participate in meetings or fulfill agreed-upon responsibilities, we should openly discuss whether that role continues to be the best fit for both the individual and the lab.

---

### Communication

Healthy research communities depend on honest and respectful communication.

When concerns arise, we encourage one another to:

- Speak directly with the people involved whenever appropriate.
- Listen before making assumptions.
- Focus on resolving problems rather than assigning blame.
- Seek understanding before seeking agreement.

Developing these communication skills is an important part of becoming a successful researcher and colleague.

---

### Celebrating One Another

Every accomplishment strengthens our community.

We celebrate publications, presentations, grants, fellowships, awards, successful defenses, new skills, and personal milestones. We recognize that every project benefits from different forms of expertise, including writing, programming, GIS, statistics, data management, visualization, literature reviews, mentoring, organization, and project coordination.

No contribution is insignificant when it advances the work of the team.

---

### Our Commitment

We are committed to building a lab where people enjoy coming each week, feel respected, and know they belong.

We believe that great science is built through collaboration, trust, generosity, and mutual respect. We will support one another, communicate openly, celebrate each other's successes, and approach our work with professionalism, gratitude, and humility.

Together, we will create meaningful research, meaningful relationships, and a community we are proud to be part of.
