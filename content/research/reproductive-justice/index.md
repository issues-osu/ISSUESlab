  <p>As part of the <strong>Investigating Spatial Structures in Urban Environments (ISSUES)</strong> lab, our work on Maternal and Child Health (MCH) is grounded in a transdisciplinary approach that links law, public health, and spatial data science. We investigate how structural inequities—such as housing instability and neighborhood disinvestment—shape health trajectories from pregnancy through childhood. A cornerstone of this work is our research on <strong>"Linking Intimate Partner Violence During Pregnancy to Infant Birthweight,"</strong> which examines the causal pathways between abuse and adverse birth outcomes.</p>

  <h2>Core Questions We Address</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>How does <strong>intimate partner violence (IPV)</strong> during pregnancy influence maternal morbidity and infant health outcomes like low birth weight?</li>
    <li>What are the spatial patterns of <strong>severe child maltreatment</strong> and child homicide, and how do they correlate with markers of structural disadvantage?</li>
    <li>How do reproductive health policies and abortion restrictions interact with social determinants to exacerbate health disparities?</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Methods and Approaches</h2>
  <p>We utilize advanced quantitative methods to rigorously analyze these complex issues:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Geospatial Machine Learning:</strong> We employ sophisticated machine learning algorithms to identify spatial non-stationarity in health outcomes.</li>
    <li><strong>PRAMS Data Analysis:</strong> Leveraging Pregnancy Risk Assessment Monitoring System (PRAMS) data to model <strong>"Latent Class Analysis of Maternal Stress,"</strong> identifying distinct risk profiles among expecting mothers.</li>
    <li><strong>Geographically Weighted Regression (GWR):</strong> Mapping how the relationships between risk factors and health outcomes vary across space.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Ongoing Projects</h2>
  <p>Current initiatives include a spatial analysis of <strong>child maltreatment hotspots</strong> in Los Angeles, an investigation into the mediating pathways of psychosocial stressors on maternal health, and the development of predictive models to better resource community-based prevention strategies. Our goal is to provide actionable evidence for policies that protect vulnerable families and promote reproductive justice.</p>
