  <p>As part of the <strong>Investigating Spatial Structures in Urban Environments (ISSUES)</strong> lab, our work on Maternal and Child Health (MCH) is grounded in a transdisciplinary approach that links law, public health, and spatial data science. We investigate how structural inequities—such as housing instability, neighborhood disinvestment, and restricted reproductive access—shape health trajectories from pregnancy through childhood.</p>

  <h2>Core Questions We Address</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>How does <strong>spatial accessibility to abortion care</strong> intersect with maternal vulnerability to shape adverse birth outcomes in the post-Dobbs landscape?</li>
    <li>How does <strong>intimate partner violence (IPV)</strong> during pregnancy influence maternal morbidity and infant health outcomes like low birth weight?</li>
    <li>What are the spatial patterns of <strong>maternal stress</strong> and how do psychosocial risk factors cluster geographically?</li>
    <li>How do reproductive health policies and abortion restrictions interact with social determinants to exacerbate health disparities?</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Methods and Approaches</h2>
  <p>We utilize advanced quantitative methods to rigorously analyze these complex issues:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>GIS & Transportation Network Modeling:</strong> Quantifying travel burden, service deserts, and spatial accessibility to abortion and reproductive healthcare using multimodal transportation networks.</li>
    <li><strong>Maternal Vulnerability Index (MVI):</strong> A novel, validated census-tract-level measure capturing multidimensional drivers of poor maternal health including healthcare access, reproductive resources, physical/mental health, environmental conditions, and socioeconomic disadvantage.</li>
    <li><strong>Latent Class Analysis:</strong> Identifying distinct risk profiles among expecting mothers using PRAMS (Pregnancy Risk Assessment Monitoring System) data.</li>
    <li><strong>Mediation Analysis:</strong> Examining pathways through which IPV affects birth outcomes via psychosocial stressors, health problems, and substance use.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Current Funded Projects</h2>
  
### Spatial Accessibility to Abortion Services & Maternal Vulnerability
  
  **Funding**: The Ohio State University, Institute for Population Research, $25,000 (2023-24)
  
  **Project**: Spatial Accessibility of Reproductive Health Care Services Following Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization
  
  **Description**: This study examines whether spatial constraints on abortion access align with community-level maternal vulnerability and how these structural conditions are associated with adverse birth outcomes. Using the novel Maternal Vulnerability Index (MVI) and GIS-based transportation network modeling, we quantify travel burden, service deserts, and spatial accessibility to abortion care in the post-Dobbs landscape.
  
  **Presentation**: [IPR Seminar Series, March 10, 2026](/event/ipr-seminar-2026/)
