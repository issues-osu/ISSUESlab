  <p>Our lab adopts a comprehensive life-course perspective to investigate the <strong>manifestation of violence across the lifespan</strong>. Guided by a <strong>Big Data for Social Justice</strong> framework, we study how structural disadvantage contributes to interpersonal violence at every stage of life—from child maltreatment and intimate partner violence to firearm-related injury and elder abuse in institutional settings.</p>

  <h2>Core Questions We Address</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>How does violence manifest differently across developmental stages, from youth bullying to domestic abuse in later life?</li>
    <li>What are the <strong>spatio-temporal patterns of firearm violence</strong>, child maltreatment, and intimate partner violence, and how do neighborhood contexts shape risk?</li>
    <li>How do structural factors like <strong>redlining, neighborhood disinvestment</strong>, and environmental conditions contribute to the cycle of violence across generations?</li>
    <li>What role does the built environment—including green space, walkability, and accessibility—play in violence prevention?</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Methods and Approaches</h2>
  <p>We utilize interdisciplinary methods to capture the complexity of violence:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Bayesian Spatial & Spatiotemporal Modeling:</strong> Mapping the trajectory of violence within communities and across time to identify persistent hotspots and protective factors.</li>
    <li><strong>Machine Learning & AI:</strong> Using structured topic modeling, random forest prediction models, and natural language processing to analyze narratives and predict risk.</li>
    <li><strong>Geospatial Analysis:</strong> Examining walkable routes to school, street-view greenness, and accessibility to understand environmental determinants of violence exposure.</li>
    <li><strong>Big Data Integration:</strong> Leveraging large administrative datasets, medical records, and crime data to monitor patterns of abuse, neglect, and injury in vulnerable populations.</li>
  </ul>
