  <p>Our lab explores the profound connection between neighborhood safety and educational opportunity. A major focus of our work is the impact of <strong>gun violence on youth</strong>, investigating how exposure to community violence and proximity to shooting incidents affects student attendance, achievement, and mental health. We move beyond the classroom to study how the broader built environment—including safe passages to school and neighborhood crime dynamics—shapes the learning experience.</p>

  <h2>Core Questions We Address</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>How does exposure to <strong>neighborhood gun violence</strong> impact academic performance and disciplinary outcomes?</li>
    <li>What are the spatial patterns of <strong>school shootings</strong> and violence near schools, and how do they respond to policy changes?</li>
    <li>How do structural inequities like exclusionary discipline and racial segregation interact with safe neighborhood conditions?</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Methods and Approaches</h2>
  <p>We apply rigorous spatial and statistical techniques to understand the educational context:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Spatial Risk Analysis:</strong> Mapping high-risk areas for violence near schools to inform Safe Passage programs and resource allocation.</li>
    <li><strong>Quasi-Experimental Designs:</strong> Evaluating the impact of safety interventions and policy reforms on student outcomes.</li>
    <li><strong>Participatory Research:</strong> Collaborating with educators and youth to design equity-focused interventions that address the root causes of violence.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Ongoing Projects</h2>
  <p>Current studies include a spatial analysis of gun violence density around K-12 schools, an evaluation of the relationship between <strong>school safety climates</strong> and student trauma responses, and research into policy reforms aimed at dismantling the school-to-prison pipeline. Our work aims to create safer, more supportive environments where all youth can thrive.</p>
