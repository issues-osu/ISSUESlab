Congratulations to **Dr. Amy Watson-Grace**, **Hexin Yang**, **Dr. Gia Elise Barboza-Salerno**, and **Dr. Karla Shockley McCarthy** on their manuscript in ***AERA Open***!

> **"Minor Infractions, Major Consequences: A Multilevel Analysis of School Discipline Disparities Affecting Native American Students Attending New Mexico Public Schools"**  
> *Amy Watson-Grace, Hexin Yang, Gia Elise Barboza-Salerno, & Karla Shockley McCarthy*  
> *AERA Open*

### Overview of the Study

Exclusionary school discipline disproportionately affects youth of color, yet the experiences of Native American and Indigenous students remain critically underexamined in quantitative educational research. Using statewide K–12 administrative data from New Mexico public schools, the research team applied multilevel modeling to examine how student race/ethnicity and infraction severity interact to shape the odds of receiving harsh exclusionary discipline (such as out-of-school suspension or referral to law enforcement) versus in-school disciplinary responses.

### Key Takeaways

- **Disproportionate Sanctions for Minor Conduct:** While the likelihood of harsh discipline increases with infraction severity across all student groups, racial disparities are sharpest at lower levels of infraction severity—where administrative discretion is greatest.
- **Systemic Inequities Facing Native American Students:** Native American students face significantly higher odds of harsh exclusionary consequences for minor and moderate infractions compared to their peers, highlighting how discretionary disciplinary practices reinforce structural inequities in public education.
- **Policy Implications:** The findings call for culturally responsive, equity-centered discipline reforms and stronger accountability around discretionary referrals for minor infractions in K–12 schools.

Congratulations to Amy, Hexin, Gia, and Karla on this important contribution to educational equity and social justice scholarship!
