This new article by **Dr. Gia E. Barboza-Salerno** argues that **Predictive Risk Models (PRMs)** can be effective when focused on *prevention*. The argument is grounded in two core principles. The first is a rights-based balancing approach that extends beyond privacy concerns to encompass due process protections tied to legally recognized status.  
The second emphasizes that PRMs, rather than serving solely as detection or risk assessment tools, can help **identify structural risk factors**, **guide equitable public investment**, and **promote accountability**, without expanding surveillance or punitive measures that compound systemic harm.  

Together, these principles challenge the false dichotomy between prevention and punishment that underlies much of AI’s deployment in family regulation. By integrating a legal and ethical framework rooted in rights protection and structural accountability, the article redefines what responsible and justice-oriented AI looks like in the context of family welfare systems.
