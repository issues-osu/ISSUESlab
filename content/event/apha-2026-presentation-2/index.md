abstract: "Neighborhood structural inequities predict mortality; however, epidemiologic research rarely integrates individual-level cause-of-death narratives with population-level structural indicators, obscuring how spatial context and place shape the distribution of mortality mechanisms. We used unsupervised k-means clustering of nineteen population-level structural indicators to construct spatially defined neighborhood typologies across Cook County, Illinois census tracts, linking typologies to geocoded medical examiner narratives (2018–2023). Four neighborhood types were identified: racially segregated/economically disinvested, ethnoracial transitional, White affluent professional, and Hispanic blue-collar. Cardiovascular disease and combined drug toxicity predominated across clusters. Disinvested neighborhoods showed higher firearm- and drug-related mortality. Models performed strongly (F1: 0.912–0.992; accuracy up to 98.6%), with 80%–85% of selected predictors derived from narrative text. Where you live not only predicts whether you die, it also shapes the spatial patterning of how death occurs."
